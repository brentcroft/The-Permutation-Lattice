# 📐 Layer 2: The 2D Box - Tallying, Coordinates, and Symmetry

**Goal:** To introduce 2D coordinate geometry by revealing how the concept of "counting paths" selects a highly constrained subset of all possible permutations—the **box indexes**. Students discover coordinate weights, cycle symmetries, and the first form of the structural twist operator (:).

## 🖼️ I. Tallying and Coordinate Constraints

### 1. Folding the Index (The Selection Process)
We move from the abstract displacement of $S_n$ to concrete, constrained paths on a grid.
*   **The Fold:** A 1D index is "folded" into a 2D box of volume $V = a \times b$. This folding process is a powerful constraint: it selects only those permutations that can be generated by a systematic, repetitive counting pattern (a stride).
*   **Tallying Conventions:** We define two complementary ways to tile the $a \times b$ box:
    *   **$(a, b)$ Tally (Example: "a lots of b"):** We stride $a$ units along the X-axis, and repeat $b$ times along the Y-axis. The path winds through coordinates $(x, y)$ in a specific order.
    *   **$(b, a)$ Tally (Example: "b lots of a"):** We stride $b$ units along the X-axis, and repeat $a$ times along the Y-axis.
*   **The Critical Constraint (Advanced Note):** The number of valid box indexes for a volume $V$ is dictated by the prime factor decomposition of $V$. The index count is dramatically smaller than $V!$, selecting only paths with highly structured, modular periodicity.

### 2. Defining the Box Index (The Result)
The **Box Index** is the specific permutation generated by a standardized, cyclic tiling pattern on the grid. It is a *sparse subset* of $S_n$. The coordinates $(x, y)$ are no longer independent labels; they are coupled by the movement pattern.

## 🌀 II. Geometry, Symmetry, and The Twist Operator

### 1. Torus Wrapping and Periodicity
*   **The Visualization:** The flat grid is a **net** of a torus with periods $a$ and $b$. Tallying traces a straight, continuous path (a geodesic) on the surface of the torus, which appears as a spiral when projected onto the plane.
*   **The Mathematical Link (Recurrence):** This winding path is equivalent to a modular recurrence: $c_{k+1} \equiv s \cdot c_k \pmod{V-1}$. The winding makes the hidden periodicity—the modular arithmetic—tangible.

### 2. Fixed Points and Symmetry (The Invariant Structure)
The geometric mechanism of counting (the `:` round-trip) guarantees structural invariants:
*   **Guaranteed Fixed Points:** The origin $(0,0)$, the terminus $(a-1, b-1)$, and **all** points lying on the main diagonal $(x, x)$ are always fixed points of the derived permutation.
*   **Cycle Symmetry:** Cycles always exhibit predictable pairings:
    1.  **Self-Symmetry:** Cycles are self-reflective across the center of the box.
    2.  **Paired Reflections:** Cycles occur in pairs, where one cycle is the precise reflection of the other through the box center.

### 3. The Twist Operator `:` (Forward $\leftrightarrow$ Backward)
The twist operator joins a forward tally path $P$ with the inverse of a reverse tally path $Q$.
*   $P:Q$ means combining the forward flow with the reverse flow. This operation is the primary mechanism for creating structural relationships between cycles and revealing hidden symmetry.

## 📏 III. Metrics and Weight Discovery

### 1. Distance Comparison: Radiance vs. Energy
We compare the 1D and 2D measurements of "displacement effort":
*   **1D Radiance ($R$):** $\Sigma|jump|$ (Total sum of absolute jumps). This measures the cumulative effort of movement along a single line.
*   **2D Energy ($E$):** $\Sigma(\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2)$. This is the sum of squared Euclidean displacements, capturing the energy required to move the structure.
*   **Deep Harmony:** For any box with adjacent dimensions, $(b, b+1)$, the cycle's Radiance $R$ is exactly equal to its Energy $E$. This is a critical geometric invariant revealing a deep underlying symmetry between linear and planar metrics.

### 2. Coordinate Magic: Weight Discovery
For any cycle, the position $k$ can be reconstructed using two separate bases (the $x$ and $y$ axes). This suggests a dual representation:
*   $c_k$ can be defined by its $x$-coordinate projection (base $t$) or its $y$-coordinate projection (base $u$).
*   The cycle position satisfies: $c_k \equiv W_t \cdot t^k \pmod{t^L - 1}$ and $c_k \equiv W_u \cdot u^{-k} \pmod{u^L - 1}$.
*   By finding the alignment weights $W_t$ and $W_u$ interactively, the student discovers that the cycle is fully defined by two separate, yet coupled, modular processes.
