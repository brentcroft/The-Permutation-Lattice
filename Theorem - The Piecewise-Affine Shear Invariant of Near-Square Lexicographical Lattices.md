## Theorem: The Piecewise-Affine Shear Invariant of Near-Square Lexicographical Lattices
Let $b \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ be a positive integer, and let $N = b(b+1)$. Let $\mathcal{L}_N = \{0, 1, 2, \dots, N-1\}$ be a finite set of linear indices, mapped bijectively to a 2D integer lattice grid $\mathcal{G} = \{(u, v) \in \mathbb{Z}^2 : 0 \le u < b, \, 0 \le v \le b\}$ via the standard row-major lexicographical indexing function:
$$\phi(u, v) = u(b+1) + v$$ 
Let $\sigma \in S_N$ be a permutation on $\mathcal{L}_N$ defined by the action of a piecewise-affine geometric shear operator $T: \mathcal{G} \to \mathcal{G}$, such that $\sigma(i) = \phi(T(\phi^{-1}(i)))$. The coordinate mapping is partitioned into two disjoint domains, $\mathcal{G} = R_1 \cup R_2$, where:
$$T(u, v) = \begin{cases} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \end{pmatrix} & \text{for } (u,v) \in R_1 : u+v < b \\ \\ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} -b \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} & \text{for } (u,v) \in R_2 : u+v \ge b \end{cases}$$ 
Define two distinct global displacement metrics on the permutation $\sigma$:

   1. The Linear $L_1$ Index Displacement (Spearman's footrule):
   $$D_{L_1}(\sigma) = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \vert{} \sigma(i) - i \vert{}$$ 
   2. The Spatial $L_2^2$ Lattice Displacement (Squared Euclidean coordinate distance):
   $$D_{L_2^2}(\sigma) = \sum_{(u,v) \in \mathcal{G}} \left\Vert{} T(u,v) - \begin{pmatrix} u \\ v \end{pmatrix} \right\Vert{}_2^2$$ 

Then, for any near-square aspect ratio dimension $b \times (b+1)$, the global displacement metrics satisfy the exact zero-sum invariant identity:
$$D_{L_1}(\sigma) - D_{L_2^2}(\sigma) = 0$$ 
------------------------------
## Structural Lemma (The Core Mechanism)
The total invariant holds true because the domain partition boundaries and the integer translation vector $\vec{c} = (-b, 1)^T$ induce a perfect cancellation of the polynomial remainders generated by the modular boundary conditions. Specifically, the summation of the linear index jumps in the 1-D array perfectly mirrors the sum of the squared physical coordinate steps across the 2-D lattice:
$$\sum_{(u,v) \in R_1} \left[ \phi(T(u,v)) - \phi(u,v) \right] + \sum_{(u,v) \in R_2} \left[ \phi(u,v) - \phi(T(u,v)) \right] = D_{L_2^2}(\sigma)$$ 
By invoking Faulhaber’s Formulas for the power sums of integers, the regional boundary errors resolve symmetrically, rendering the difference between the 1-D and 2-D global metrics identically zero. ■
